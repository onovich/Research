01

Conclusion first

Read the sample coverage and recalculated metrics before the project hypotheses.

What was actually observed?

The median reported monthly revenue is $23K across eight selected cases, with a fourfold range. These are founder-reported figures from different interview dates and describe only the selected successful cases.

How complete is the profit evidence?

Five of eight cases contain some profit statement, but only three disclose a margin. Revenue rankings therefore cannot substitute for profit comparisons; missing margins remain unknown.

Which hypothesis has the most complete evidence?

A narrow workflow tool inside an established platform. Data Fetcher supplies revenue, 600 paid accounts, an 85% margin, and major costs. That supports a first experiment, not a success-rate estimate.

Recommended starting sentence

For one professional role using one established platform or spreadsheet, automate one repeated job so they improve one measurable outcome. Charge for repeated use or saved labor.

Observation
Profit AI’s founder described building the product with AI tools without prior coding ability; the same case disclosed a thin paid-user base and churn.
Inference
AI lowers the first-build barrier for a known workflow; the cases do not show that it improves acquisition, retention, or unit economics.
Unknown
Public cases cannot establish whether an AI-built product remains secure, reliable, maintainable, or profitable without qualified review.
Low-fit first projects

Generic AI chat or writing tools, two-sided marketplaces, sensitive financial/medical/legal automation, SEO-only acquisition bets, and APIs that require perfect uptime before the first customer.

05

Validate before you automate

The goal is not to finish an app in four weeks. It is to earn enough evidence to justify the app.

Week 1

Prove the repeated pain

  • Interview 10 people in one role using the same platform or spreadsheet.
  • Collect five redacted input/output pairs.
  • Stop if the job is rare, low-value, regulated beyond your competence, or already solved natively.
Week 2

Sell the manual outcome

  • Deliver the result manually or with a private script.
  • Ask for a paid pilot before building a dashboard.
  • Continue only if two users pay, commit budget, or introduce the budget owner.
Week 3

Build one end-to-end job

  • One login, one input, one successful output, one payment path, basic logs.
  • Use AI coding tools for scaffolding; derive explicit acceptance tests from real examples.
  • Do not add teams, a mobile client, open-ended chat, or a second integration.
Week 4

Test repeat use and support cost

  • Measure activation, successful jobs, repeat use, support minutes, and gross margin.
  • Stop or redesign if exceptions require constant founder repair or one customer consumes the margin.
  • Add a second integration only after the first retains paying users.
