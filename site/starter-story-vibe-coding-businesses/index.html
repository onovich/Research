Starter Story online business study

Across 40 public data pages, 349 deduplicated projects have a $30K displayed monthly-revenue median; selected success cases, sparse profit data, and a missing denominator for failed attempts prevent these figures from estimating startup success rates.

This report examines the platform's displayed revenue distribution, the available profit evidence, and the technical boundary between fully online delivery and AI-assisted development.

Background
Starter Story is centered on businesses that report revenue. It can show visible business forms, but it does not represent all startup attempts.
Question
What revenue distribution is visible, how much profit evidence is available, and which structures can be delivered online with AI-assisted development?

01

Key findings

The public page family describes visible revenue; the case review adds profit, cost, and operating constraints.

The public evidence spans thousands of pages, but it is not a population sample

The sitemap contains 19,122 URLs, including 3,264 story URLs, 477 business URLs, and 40 data pages. The homepage separately claims 2,997+ revenue-generating projects. These are site and platform counts, not attempted-startup denominators.

Displayed monthly revenue has a $30K median and a wide distribution

All 40 public data pages expose 677 row occurrences and 349 unique projects; 292 show monthly revenue. Their displayed median is $30K, with a $9K–$113.5K interquartile range. The excerpt is curated and cannot forecast a new project.

Profit disclosure is substantially less complete than revenue disclosure

Five of eight reviewed software cases contain some profit statement, but only three disclose a margin. Data Fetcher provides the most complete observed record because it includes revenue, paid accounts, named costs, and a stated margin.

Overlapping labels do not form a mutually exclusive taxonomy

Cross-category duplicates account for 48.4% of public row occurrences, and 36.7% of unique projects appear on more than one data page. Category cards are useful discovery aids; their counts and medians are not mutually exclusive business-model statistics.

Most public records show revenue, but the distribution belongs to selected winners

Of 349 unique public projects, 292 expose a monthly-revenue field. Within those 292 records, 74.3% display at least $10K/month, 51.7% at least $30K, 29.5% at least $100K, and 4.5% at least $1M.

The middle half spans more than twelvefold

The 25th and 75th percentiles are $9K and $113.5K/month. The wide spread makes a single mean or headline case especially misleading.

Nearly half of row occurrences are duplicates

Deduplication removes 328 of 677 row occurrences. Another 128 of 349 unique projects appear on more than one public data page.

−25%

The estimates are sensitive to page selection

Under the same parsing and deduplication rules, the ten-page thematic subset has a $40K median and the 40-page family has a $30K median. This robustness test shows that page selection materially affects the center of the distribution.

Solopreneur Score does not rise with displayed revenue

Among 291 projects with both fields, the Pearson correlation between the platform score and log monthly revenue is −0.189. Score-band medians are $85K below 60, $35K at 60–69.9, $10.5K at 70–79.9, and $26K at 80+. The public sample does not support treating this score as a revenue forecast.

Page-label proxies support delivery comparisons, but they remain overlapping

A 13-page software-delivery union contains 159 unique projects, including 135 with revenue and a $24.8K/month median. A stricter seven-page low-complexity union contains 90 projects; its 73 revenue-known records have a $19.4K median and a $6K–$50K interquartile range. Fifteen still display at least $100K/month.

Observed
Profit AI’s founder described shipping paid software with AI tools despite no prior coding ability; the same case reported churn and a small paid-user base.
Supported inference
AI can reduce the effort needed for a first version of a known workflow.
Not established
The cases do not show that AI improves demand, retention, security, reliability, maintainability, or profit.
Implementation complexity is not equivalent to operating burden

A small interface can still sit on top of expensive acquisition, high churn, sensitive data, two-sided operations, or continuous support. The public data measure the build layer poorly and the operating layer even less completely.

05

Evidence boundaries

These missing denominators prevent the public winner records from becoming a success-rate, profit-rate, or clean model-comparison dataset.

Failed-project denominator
The database does not expose a comparable population of attempts that earned little or nothing, so no success probability can be calculated.
Random sampling
Default public rows are curated and ordered by the platform. Their percentiles describe the visible excerpt only.
Current verifiable profitability
Interview figures are founder-reported snapshots from different dates; most records do not provide current financial statements or a disclosed margin.
Mutually exclusive categories
36.7% of unique excerpt projects appear on more than one public data page, and some labels include visibly different business types.
Founder labor and support burden
Reported revenue rarely captures unpaid founder time, exception handling, support intensity, or maintenance cost.
Causal effect of AI coding
The cases show that some founders used AI tools. They do not isolate whether AI improved acquisition, retention, product quality, or profit.
Distribution transferability
A platform store, SEO position, audience, or partnership may be case-specific and cannot be assumed to transfer to another product.
Long-term retention
Only a minority disclose churn or cohort behavior, so reported monthly revenue does not by itself show durability.
06

Data sources and research limitations

The site inventory, aggregate cards, public sample, and reviewed cases answer different questions and remain separately labelled.

Site inventory
One sitemap snapshot was decompressed and counted: 19,122 URLs, including 3,264 story URLs, 477 business URLs, and 40 data pages.
Excerpt collection
42 single-threaded GET requests: one sitemap, one homepage, and all 40 public data pages, six seconds apart, with an identifying research user agent.
Access boundary
No login, pagination guessing, or access-control bypass. No 403, 429, or challenge response occurred.
Publication boundary
Only aggregate calculations and direct source links are published. The row-level extraction remains local and is not part of the public repository.

Research limitations

The 349-project excerpt covers the full public data-page family, but it remains a default-ordered sample of selected winners. Every business figure is a public founder statement or Starter Story display from a particular date. The report cannot verify net income, taxes, founder labor, current churn, or the probability that a new entrant reproduces a case.
