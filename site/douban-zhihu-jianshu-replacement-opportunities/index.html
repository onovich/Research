Platform strategy field study · 2026

Old web, new wedges

What Douban, Zhihu, and Jianshu still do well—and where an independent developer can build without cloning them.

Their interfaces are reproducible. Their accumulated objects, answers, identities, search behavior, norms, and governance history are not. The small-team opportunity is to unbundle one valuable function for one dense community.

Horizontal clone
No-go for a solo developer
Best wedge
Vertical objects + credible people + recurring work
Key constraint
Cold start, governance, and distribution—not feature implementation
Read the verdict Use the six-week test

Brief mode: the verdict, opportunity ranking, and validation plan stay visible while evidence and sources are condensed.

01

Do not replace them. Unbundle them.

The strongest opportunity is a bounded vertical product that becomes useful before it becomes social.

Can an indie developer replace them?

Not as a general destination. A feature clone starts with no corpus, no reputation, no discovery demand, no norms, and no reason to migrate years of history.

Is there a startup opportunity?

Yes, but as a wedge. Serve one vertical object set, one trusted contributor group, or one recurring community workflow. Sell the work or data before opening a public feed.

Why has nobody taken the whole market?

Because the market fragmented instead. Publishing, chat, video, reviews, paid communities, and personal archives spread across larger platforms and private groups; creators commonly multi-home.

Recommended product sentence

For one expert or enthusiast community, maintain a trusted object database and automate one repeated compare, shortlist, review, submit, procure, or report job.

The database is not free seed capital

Douban’s legal statement covers object metadata, scores, reviews, counts, marks, topics, and groups, and prohibits unauthorized extraction and derivative services. Build with licensed, public-domain, first-party, or user-consented data.

03

Why no one replaced them

The code is the easy layer. Accumulated state, coordinated adoption, and governance are the product.

01 · Accumulated state
Objects, answers, links, trusted identities, search behavior, moderator decisions, and community vocabulary cannot be shipped in a sprint.
02 · Cold start
Contributors need worthwhile demand; readers need useful supply; reviews need objects; objects need trustworthy structure.
03 · Fragmentation
Users already publish, chat, review, watch, and organize across large networks, newsletters, and private groups. There is less demand for one universal successor.
04 · Community economics
Ads require scale, subscriptions require unique recurring value, and open discussion creates support, moderation, trust, and dispute costs.
05 · Governance
Public UGC can require identity, account, content, comment, complaint, monitoring, and emergency-response systems. Scope-specific professional advice is necessary.
06 · Slow utility
A stable archive, familiar reputation signal, and slow-moving norm can retain value without constant product novelty.
Broad community is not lightweight SaaS

Zhihu reported RMB 2.749B in 2025 revenue, RMB 195.2M GAAP net loss, RMB 1.252B sales and marketing expense, and RMB 525M R&D expense. Even at scale, the operating model is heavy.

04

Opportunity map

Start with single-player or small-group utility. Add the public community only after return behavior exists.

01

Vertical object + expert network

Strongest fit. Bound the corpus, seed credible supply, and monetize comparison, shortlist, procurement, submission, or reporting work.

02

Private community operations

Serve clubs, associations, cohorts, and creator communities with permissions, archives, events, reviews, and exports—without a public-feed cold start.

03

Creator-owned publishing stack

Combine website, email, archive, paid membership, export, and migration. The paid outcome is ownership and direct reach, not another generic editor.

04

Personal library and curation layer

Begin with user-consented URLs, exports, RSS, bookmarks, and notes. Single-player value lowers cold-start risk; subscription willingness must be tested.

05

Moderation and reputation infrastructure

Sell queues, evidence trails, contribution quality, roles, and escalation tools to communities that already have an audience.

Vibe coding helps with the shell

AI can compress CRUD, authentication, search UI, payments, notifications, admin tools, normalization, and moderation queues. It cannot create licensed data, credible contributors, norms, distribution, retention, or production judgment.

05

Six weeks to test the wedge

Earn the right to add a community layer by first proving a repeated job, trusted data, and return behavior.

Week 1

Choose the object and job

  • Interview 12 people in one niche.
  • Collect the objects, files, links, and spreadsheets they actually use.
  • Stop if there is no repeated compare, coordinate, or report job.
Week 2

Seed the corpus manually

  • Create 100 verified object records and a transparent data policy.
  • Use licensed, public-domain, first-party, or consented data.
  • Test whether five users return without a social feed.
Week 3

Recruit credible supply

  • Invite 10 domain contributors.
  • Ask each for one structured review, checklist, or comparison.
  • Agree attribution, ownership, export, and moderation rules.
Week 4

Sell the workflow

  • Offer a paid workspace, export, alert, shortlist, or report.
  • Continue only if three users or one organization commits budget.
  • Do not open public posting yet.
Week 5

Build one bounded product

  • One object model, one workflow, one permission model.
  • One export path and one audit trail.
  • Instrument successful jobs, support time, and failed records.
Week 6

Measure return behavior

  • Track second-week return, useful contributions, and moderation minutes.
  • Open sharing only when it improves acquisition without lowering quality.
  • Stop if founder repair consumes the margin.
Do not build first

A general public feed, an unauthorized Douban mirror, an ad-funded community, an AI content farm, open discussion in a sensitive domain, or a creator platform with publishing but no ownership or paid outcome.
