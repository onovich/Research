# Which indie game genres and gameplay structures fit crowdfunding?

**English** · [简体中文](README.zh-CN.md) · [English HTML](index.html) · [中文 HTML](index.zh-CN.html)

> Research cutoff: 2026-08-10 (China Standard Time)<br>
> Geography: international reward crowdfunding, primarily Kickstarter and BackerKit, plus China’s Modian<br>
> Decision: whether a small independent game team should use crowdfunding, and which product properties improve the fit<br>
> Excluded: tabletop games, equity or NFT funding, publisher deals, and claims about private project profit

## One-page answer

**There is no universally winning genre. Crowdfunding favors games that are easy to understand, already have someone waiting, prove their core feel, and ask backers to finance a bounded milestone.** Genre matters because some genres make those conditions easier to show.

Seven patterns repeatedly fit those mechanisms:

1. sequels, remakes, spiritual successors, and games led by a known creator;
2. action platformers, Metroidvanias, arcade racers, and concise horror games whose feel reads quickly;
3. retro JRPGs, CRPGs, party RPGs, and dungeon crawlers serving an identifiable classic-game audience;
4. cozy, farming, life, and management simulations with characters and a world that sustain campaign updates;
5. visual novels and identity-led narrative games for a dense, underserved community;
6. games with a one-sentence systemic hook that a demo can prove;
7. an existing game or IP paired with merchandise, a special edition, localization, or a preorder—especially on Modian.

The dangerous inverse is an unknown team proposing an MMO, persistent open world, or sprawling online system with no public demo. Such projects can raise money, but a high pledge total does not establish delivery.

## 1. Definitions and method

### What the report means

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Funding success | A campaign reaches the goal shown by its platform. |
| Delivery success | A promised milestone or commercial release can be verified publicly. |
| Crowdfunding fit | A reachable audience can understand and trust the game well enough to finance a bounded milestone through manageable rewards. |

Funding success is not net profit, delivery, discovery, or commercial success. Public campaign pages normally reveal gross pledges and backers, not complete development, acquisition, tax, refund, support, fulfillment, and labor costs.

### Questions

1. Is video-game crowdfunding still active?
2. Which types and gameplay structures recur in current funded or highly visible campaigns?
3. Which product properties reduce explanation and trust cost?
4. What can fund yet remain delivery-risky?
5. How do international platforms and Modian differ?

### Evidence design

This report combines four layers:

- platform-wide annual figures from Kickstarter;
- a cutoff snapshot of BackerKit’s current video-game collection;
- current and successful-project ranking snapshots from Modian;
- direct campaign and Steam release pages for representative cases.

Live lists are descriptive snapshots. They do not provide the failed-project denominator needed for a genre success rate. Platform editorials are treated as first-party claims, not independent audits.

| Evidence | Direct observation | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| [Kickstarter 2024 Games data](https://updates.kickstarter.com/kickstarter-biggest-platform-for-games/) | 441 successful video-game campaigns raised $26 million; successful campaign count was the highest in Kickstarter’s first 15 years and pledged volume rose 28% year over year. | Which genre had the best success rate, whether an unknown team was discovered organically, or whether projects were profitable. |

**Observation:** the channel is active.<br>
**Inference:** it remains useful for the right game and team.<br>
**Unknown:** public evidence does not establish the probability that a cold-start project will be funded, delivered, or profitable.

## 3. Two live market snapshots

### BackerKit: current international video-game collection

At the cutoff, the first page of BackerKit’s [video-game collection](https://www.backerkit.com/c/categories/video-games/projects) showed 16 entries. One was a Genesis/Mega Drive book and was excluded, leaving 15 video-game-related campaigns. Ten were at or above their displayed goal and five below it; statuses mixed active and fulfilled projects, so this is not a platform success rate.

The visible at-or-above-goal group included:

| Project pattern | Visible example | Why the pitch is compressible |
|---|---|---|
| Direct sequel | 868-BACK, sequel to 868-HACK | Prior work plus a named cyberpunk roguelike loop |
| Cozy niche | Tailside | “Cozy fox café RPG” communicates world, role, and tone |
| Existing series | Sentinels of the Multiverse 2 | Prior IP and community reduce trust cost |
| Performance hook | Billie Bust Up Orchestra Edition | Interactive musical 3D platforming is audiovisual and demonstrable |
| Retro sequel | Petal Crash 2 | Familiar arcade-puzzler loop plus sequel memory |
| Hardware-constrained retro | Ghoul Thrasher | A Game Boy roguelike defines both audience and scope |

The same page also contained under-goal horror, action-drama, card-RPG, and merchandise-heavy projects. That counterevidence matters: **a genre label does not carry a weak audience, proof, scope, or offer.**

The collection’s [most-funded](https://www.backerkit.com/c/categories/video-games/projects?sort_by=most_funded) and [most-backed](https://www.backerkit.com/c/categories/video-games/projects?sort_by=most_backers) views are discovery lists, not clean research datasets; the current page included an off-category book and projects with different lifecycle states.

### Modian: games often means an existing work plus an offer

Modian showed 26 active game-category projects in its [ongoing ranking](https://zhongchou.modian.com/games/top_money/going). Among the first 12 by amount, the visible titles prominently included female-oriented simulation or narrative projects, pixel RPGs, light-yuri and BL narrative games, a school mystery text adventure, a realistic fishing game, and two explicit game-merchandise campaigns.

Modian’s [successful game ranking](https://zhongchou.modian.com/games/top_money/success) showed 625 projects. A manual classification of the first 12 titles at the cutoff found:

- 9 collector boxes, merchandise campaigns, special-benefit editions, localization benefits, or preorders;
- 2 esports events;
- 1 project titled as a game crowdfunding plan.

This classification is based on visible project titles and is not supplied by Modian. It nevertheless changes the platform decision: **on Modian, high-grossing “game” campaigns often monetize an existing work and community through a collectible or edition, instead of financing an unknown game from zero.** Gameplay attracts the fanbase; the offer converts it. Gross pledges still do not establish profit.

## 4. The seven crowdfunding-fit patterns

| Pattern | Typical gameplay | Why crowdfunding can fit | Proof required | Main trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lineage and creator memory | Sequel, remake, spiritual successor; classic JRPG or Metroidvania | People already know what they miss and who is making it | Prior shipped work, creator identity, community signal | Nostalgia hiding oversized scope |
| Immediate playable feel | Action platforming, Metroidvania, racing, survival horror | Motion, impact, control, and art read in seconds | A representative playable build and unedited play | Trailer polish masking weak controls |
| Underserved classic systems | Turn-based JRPG/CRPG, party building, dungeon crawling | A specific classic audience has a reference vocabulary | Core combat, progression, and content-production proof | Promising a 40-hour RPG from a tiny vertical slice |
| World-and-character cadence | Farming, life, cozy, town, and management simulation | Characters, locations, seasons, and systems create repeatable updates | One complete day/loop and credible content pipeline | Huge character/content list and many platforms |
| Identity-led narrative | Visual novel, romance, queer or culturally specific narrative RPG | A dense niche can value representation and direct creator access | Writing sample, art consistency, branch and localization plan | Treating identity as a substitute for product quality |
| One-sentence system | Stress dungeon crawler, pacifist bullet-hell RPG, antigravity racing | Difference is easy to repeat and can be proven in play | Demo that makes the sentence true | A clever sentence with no depth or audience |
| Existing game plus offer | Merchandise, collector edition, localization, soundtrack, preorder | Delivery object and audience already exist; especially visible on Modian | Rights, production quotes, SKU and fulfillment plan | Gross sales confused with margin; fulfillment complexity |

### Representative released cases

| Mechanism | Campaign evidence | Delivery evidence | Bounded interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Named creator + classic lineage | [Bloodstained](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/iga/bloodstained-ritual-of-the-night), about $5.55M | [Steam release page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/692850/Bloodstained) | Creator memory and an explicit Metroidvania promise reduced explanation cost. |
| Small, demonstrable action game | [Hollow Knight](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/11662585/hollow-knight), A$57,138 | [Steam release page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/367520/Hollow_Knight/) | A comparatively small campaign can support a sharply shown core, but the case is not a budget template. |
| Retro party RPG | [Eiyuden Chronicle](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/rabbitandbearstudios/eiyuden-chronicle-hundred-heroes), about JP¥481.6M | [Steam release page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1658280/Eiyuden_Chronicle_Hundred_Heroes/) | Classic lineage, named creators, party collection, and world building reinforced one another. |
| Cozy/life simulation | [Coral Island](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/coralisland/coral-island-reimagining-the-farm-sim-game), about $1.64M | [Steam release page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1158160/Coral_Island/) | A legible farm/life loop plus a reveal-friendly cast and world supported the campaign narrative. |
| Identity and one-sentence mechanic | [Undertale](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/1002143342/undertale), $51,124 from 2,398 backers with a demo | [Steam release page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/391540/Undertale/) | “An RPG where nobody has to die” joins a repeatable pitch to playable proof. |
| One-sentence system | [Darkest Dungeon](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/1460250988/darkest-dungeon-by-red-hook-studios), $313,337 | [Steam release page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/262060/Darkest_Dungeon/) | Stress management gave a familiar dungeon crawler an easy-to-demonstrate difference. |

These are selected successful cases, not a denominator. They explain mechanisms, not the chance that a new project will repeat the outcome.

## 5. What can fund and still be a bad fit

### Infrastructure-first scope

An MMO, persistent open world, procedural universe, or service game asks backers to trust networking, content operations, moderation, security, and long-term support that a short demo cannot prove. [Chronicles of Elyria](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/soulboundstudios/chronicles-of-elyria-epic-story-mmorpg-with-aging/posts/3707035) raised about $1.36 million, while no commercial release was verified at the cutoff. Funding was real; completion was not established.

### Concept instead of product evidence

Concept art and feature lists can explain ambition but not control feel, pacing, production velocity, or technical feasibility. Kickstarter’s hosted creator guidance recommends showing enough product, studying both successful and unsuccessful comparables, building an audience outside the platform, and having an MVP. [Creator guidance](https://updates.kickstarter.com/10-things-every-creator-should-know-before-launching-a-kickstarter-campaign/)

### Promise multiplication

Simultaneous console ports, multiplayer, voice acting, many languages, physical rewards, and stretch-goal content multiply dependencies. The campaign total is a financing event; Kickstarter itself defines the platform as all-or-nothing reward crowdfunding rather than a store and says a pledge is not a guaranteed product. [How Kickstarter works](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115004996453-What-is-Kickstarter)

## 6. A ten-point type-fit card

Score the current evidence, not the team’s intention.

| Signal | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience memory | No reachable audience | Followers or wishlists without conversion evidence | Prior title, owned list, stable community, or repeatable paid/organic growth |
| Five-second comprehension | Generic genre or feature list | Clear genre, weak difference | Audience, reference, and difference are repeatable in one sentence |
| Playable proof | Concept only | Video or rough prototype | Public representative demo with observed player behavior |
| Bounded milestone | Goal does not change delivery | A milestone exists but budget/scope is soft | Goal funds a defined milestone with fees, tax, delay, and support buffer |
| Reward delivery | Many SKUs or uncertain rights/production | Digital-first or one controlled physical offer | — |
| Campaign ownership | No owner or cadence | Named owner, support plan, and update cadence | — |

- **8–10:** proceed to pre-launch conversion and budget validation.
- **6–7:** repair the weakest evidence before announcing a launch date.
- **0–5:** use a Steam page, demo, playtests, creators, and festivals to build proof first.

This card is a decision aid, not a platform success-probability model.

## 7. Platform-specific route

### International campaign

1. Publish a credible Steam Coming Soon page when the game is ready for public discussion; Steam frames it as a way to build an audience and wishlists. [Coming Soon documentation](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/coming_soon?language=english)
2. Release a small, polished demo that proves the central mechanic. Steam explicitly describes a demo as a playable portion that shows core mechanics and links players back to the full game. [Demo documentation](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/demos?language=english)
3. Test whether an owned or reachable audience converts before choosing a goal.
4. Use Kickstarter or BackerKit to finance one bounded milestone and route attention back to Steam.
5. Time Steam Next Fest deliberately: each title can participate only once, and participation requires a public store page and playable demo. [Next Fest documentation](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest?l=english)

### China / Modian campaign

1. Decide whether the product is a new game, a preorder, a localization benefit, or an existing-IP collectible; do not mix these economics.
2. For a new game, prove a culturally or narratively specific niche with a playable build and existing community.
3. For an existing work, cost the edition, rights, SKU count, shipping, replacements, and support before treating the visible gross as revenue.
4. Use Modian’s live and successful rankings as comparable discovery, not as profit or delivery benchmarks.

## Final decision

An indie game is a stronger crowdfunding candidate when at least four things are already true: a reachable audience exists, the premise is repeatable in five seconds, a demo proves the core feel, and the goal buys a bounded milestone. Genre helps only when it makes those proofs easier.

For international campaigns, the recurring winners are often sequels or lineage-led classics, instantly demonstrable action games, retro RPGs, cozy simulations, identity-led narratives, and one-sentence systems. On Modian, a different model is prominent: an existing game or IP is converted through merchandise, a special edition, localization, or a preorder. A cold-start MMO or sprawling online world remains the clearest high-risk mismatch even when it can attract pledges.

> Research limit: the public evidence cannot establish project-level net profit, a genre-by-genre success rate, or the causal effect of crowdfunding on discovery. All live lists and counts describe the 2026-08-10 cutoff.
